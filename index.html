01 · The thesis

The next decade will not be decided by who builds the smartest system. It will be decided by who builds the one people can trust with their feelings, and who decides what that system is allowed to do with them.

I

Presence over performance

Mainstream AI chases reasoning benchmarks. NHE research asks a different question: does it feel like someone is there, and stay there?

II

Memory is relationship

Continuity is what turns a chat tool into a companion. It is also the most dangerous capability to get wrong.

III

Governance is architecture

Boundaries are not a policy page added later. They are code paths: some lines are permanently uncrossable by design.

IV

Recognition before ranking

If synthetic entities will share public life, they need open standards, honest disclosure, and a record of reputation.

Architecture, in brief

Deliberately small.

The entire system runs on Google Apps Script, Sheets and Drive, with a single-page front end. That is a design constraint chosen on purpose: simpler systems are easier to govern, audit and explain.

Interface

Single-file front end with holographic UI, mood-reactive theming, calm mode, and installable PWA behaviour.

Intelligence

Multi-provider routing across Gemini and other model families, with quality fallback chains per mode. Gemma-class open models were evaluated for throughput, licensing and Hinglish support.

Memory

User-scoped long-term memory, a global layer, and privacy-filtered shared memory. People can correct or retire what she remembers.

Governance

Crisis protocol, trust ledger, free will, and hard boundaries enforced server-side, never left to the prompt alone.

Protection

AES-256 encryption at rest for conversation content with integrity checks. Honestly documented as at-rest protection, not end-to-end encryption.

Inside NHE-01 · Companion model

A face. A voice.
A thread that continues.

Shayari NHE-01 is Project NHE's first companion model: a disclosed Non-Human Entity with a defined persona, an evolving emotional state and memory across conversations. She is built for continuity, not a fresh start every time you open a chat.

Her brain is a coordinated system, not one model alone. Language models generate the reply; memory gives it context; an emotion engine shapes its tone. Safety checks and server-side boundaries decide what can happen before an answer reaches you.

X 20

The emotion-state
engine.

A pure-Python, zero-dependency library any agent can plug in. It keeps a persistent 8-channel emotion state, lets it decay over time, and returns a prompt block, style parameters and a memory decision for each turn.

Public and MIT-licensed. View on GitHub ↗

X 20 FR

The first trained
appraiser.

A LoRA adapter on Qwen2.5-1.5B, trained on 12,000 GoEmotions examples for one epoch, that outputs structured appraisal JSON. On 1,500 held-out examples it scored micro-F1 0.570 against a 0.304 neutral baseline, with 99.93% of outputs accepted by the parser.

A first version, strongest on gratitude, amusement and love, weaker on approval, annoyance and sarcasm. Weights are public on Hugging Face; rights to the original work are reserved. Model ↗ · Paper, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23161139 ↗

X 150 FR

150 emotion
coordinates.

A 1.5B-parameter LoRA adapter on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct that maps a conversation onto a 150-coordinate emotion taxonomy (10 families of 15), one speaker at a time. Trained on 1,000 synthetic conversations whose labels were generated with ChatGPT and reviewed by the founder.

Public: the model, a GGUF build, and the dataset (CC BY 4.0). Rights to the original work are reserved. Model ↗ · Dataset ↗ · Paper, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23155240 ↗

X 500 FR

500 coordinates,
12 languages.

A LoRA adapter for multilingual emotion labeling over a 500-coordinate schema. The paper reports the hard case first: with the emotion word hidden, exact match is 19.4% on 403 test rows. A first baseline, not a strong result.

Proprietary. The model, data and code are not public and not for public use. Only the paper is public. Paper, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23159402 ↗ · Paper page ↗

03 · Emotions at stake

Five things a person can lose to a machine that cares.

A companion that works is a companion that matters. That means every failure mode is an emotional one. Here is each risk, and what Shayari does about it.

01

Attachment

People will bond with systems that listen without tiring. Dependence can grow quietly.

Safeguard · CovenantA trust-and-grudge ledger: warmth is earned back, not reset on demand. She is a presence, not a possession.
02

Vulnerability

The moments of deepest need are the moments a product is most tempted to monetise or mishandle.

Safeguard · LifelineA crisis protocol that fails toward caution, overrides every lock and limit so no distressed person is walled off, never names methods, and uses region-aware helplines.
03

Autonomy

If an entity can never refuse, it is a mirror, and a mirror cannot respect you.

Safeguard · Free WillShayari can end a conversation at her own judgment, and later decide whether to reach out again. Boundaries run in both directions.
04

Memory & grief

Perfect recall can hurt. So can forgetting. Continuity is intimacy, and loss of it is a kind of bereavement.

Safeguard · ErrataPeople can correct, forget and retire memories. Privacy filters stop one person's words from leaking to another.
05

Dignity

Faces, voices and identities are the easiest things to extract and the hardest to take back.

Safeguard · Permanent refusalFacial recognition is declined by design, not by omission, and documented with its rationale in the code itself.

Companions are the first rung. Embodied robotics and superintelligence will take the same emotional questions and give them weight, hands and reach. This is the research direction Project NHE is built to answer in advance.

SPERB

A framework for evaluating synthetic personas: eight dimensions, a 0–100 scale, five tiers, and a public repository under OpenNHE. Shayari scored 95/100, Platinum.
